# References and attribution

1. Sara Billey, Matthew Fahrbach, Alan Talmage, *Coefficients and roots of peak polynomials*, Experimental Mathematics 25(2) (2016), 165-175. [arXiv:1410.8506v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.8506v2); [DOI:10.1080/10586458.2015.1051193](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2015.1051193). Conjecture 6.6 is the target. Corollary 3.1, Lemmas 3.2 and 4.2, and Theorem 4.3 provide earlier recurrence, integer-value, and finite-difference results. Theorem 4.3 is explicitly credited at the corresponding identity in the manuscript.
2. Alexander Diaz-Lopez, Pamela E. Harris, Erik Insko, Mohamed Omar, *A proof of the peak polynomial positivity conjecture*, Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A 149 (2017), 21-29. [arXiv:1605.01708](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01708). Theorem 1.2 establishes forward-difference positivity. That theorem is not the last-real-root claim and is not invoked as a premise of the proof here.
3. Alex Chengyu Li, *Reflected Newton Transforms and Concavity of Peak-Polynomial Coefficients*, working paper, version 3.0, 18 September 2026. [Fixed DOI:10.5281/zenodo.22832569](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22832569). The full nine-page text was consulted for this edition. Equation (2) and Theorem 1.1 give positive symmetric coefficients in the rising-binomial basis. Their direct consequences include right-side positivity, the simple largest-peak root, negativity on the last unit interval, the positive symmetric D transform, and the strict complete-monotonicity consequence. These are credited as prior implications, although their self-contained proofs are retained.

The cited coefficient theorem does not, by its immediate termwise sign argument, cover the remaining noninteger interval between the penultimate peak and the final unit interval. The manuscript supplies the further uniform sign analysis. This comparison is bounded: it neither proves that no other source covers that range nor certifies worldwide priority.
